Reflections

Reflection 1: Group Project Experience

During my intensive second year, I actively participated in a challenging group project for the Web Technologies module where we successfully developed a comprehensive full-stack e-commerce prototype from scratch. The dedicated team consisted of four members with widely varying levels of programming experience. Throughout this journey, I learned exactly how to efficiently divide responsibilities based on individual strengths and how to seamlessly merge code using Git without introducing destructive conflicts. Effective, transparent communication within the team quickly proved to be just as crucial and important as our underlying technical skills.

Coordinating suitable meeting times was significantly difficult as team members had drastically different, conflicting lab schedules. Furthermore, integrating individually written software modules also caused unexpected compatibility issues in the early stages of development. To overcome this, we promptly adopted a shared Trello board for granular task tracking and held brief, highly focused weekly stand-up meetings online. I also took the initiative to set up a robust Git branching strategy to effectively streamline our continuous code integration pipeline. Moving forward, I would greatly like to improve my project leadership skills and learn extensively more about modern Agile methodologies to manage complex, team-based software engineering projects much more efficiently and effectively in the future.

Reflection 2: Laboratory Sessions & Practical Work

Extensive lab sessions throughout my foundational first and second years, particularly in the highly technical domains of Sensor Technology and Embedded Systems, were genuinely transformative in successfully connecting abstract theory with tangible, real-world practice. I diligently developed strong hands-on competency with essential diagnostic equipment like digital oscilloscopes, multimeters, and various advanced microcontroller boards. Actively troubleshooting faulty electronic circuits during these intensive sessions fundamentally sharpened my critical, analytical thinking and problem-solving abilities considerably.

Unexpected hardware component failures during several critical, time-sensitive experiments frequently led to necessary repeated trials and significant time pressure. Initially, this steep learning curve was quite frustrating and momentarily affected my personal confidence. To counter this, I immediately started pre-reading complex lab manuals thoroughly beforehand and meticulously double-checking all intricate circuit connections before initially powering up the systems. I also firmly built a proactive habit of always saving preliminary simulation results in Proteus before ever moving forward to actual physical hardware assembly. Looking ahead, I enthusiastically aim to build a fully equipped personal electronics project bench at home to deliberately practise my skills regularly, far outside the constraints of traditionally timetabled university lab sessions.

Reflection 3: Communication & Presentation Skills – SPI Module

The Social and Professional Issues module challenged me to present technical content to a non-technical audience and participate in impromptu speaking exercises that tested my ability to think on the spot. I developed clarity in expressing complex technical ideas through structured, audience-appropriate language. I also learned to use visual aids more effectively to support my arguments.

Nervousness during impromptu tasks often affected my fluency and logical flow. Speaking in front of a large group was particularly challenging in the early weeks. I practiced speaking in front of mirrors and small peer groups to build confidence. The constructive feedback from my friends helped me identify specific areas to improve, such as pacing and eye contact. I intend to join a public speaking club and pursue additional workshops on technical communication to further refine my presentation delivery and academic writing skills.
