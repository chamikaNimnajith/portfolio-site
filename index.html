Reflections

Reflection 1: Group Project Experience

In my rigorous second year, I was actively involved in the highly engaging and challenging group project for the module called Web Technologies where our team managed to create a complete stack of the e-commerce prototype from scratch. Our committed team comprised four members possessing an extremely diverse programming background. In this process, I got familiarized with the perfect way of delegating tasks according to personal strengths and merging code using Git effectively without causing destructive merges. Communication within the group turned out to be as critical and important as the programming skills we had. Scheduling appropriate time for the meeting proved to be rather complicated since members had highly contrasting lab hours. Moreover, combining independently developed software modules also raised a lot of compatibility issues during the early stage of development. To resolve all such concerns, we immediately decided to use a Trello board for precise task allocation and conducting weekly brief online standup meetings. In addition, I initiated the process of creating a good Git branching policy for a successful code integration process. Going further, I would highly like to enhance my capabilities as a leader along with acquiring extensive knowledge regarding the Agile methodology.

Reflection 2: Laboratory Sessions & Practical Work

My laboratory hours spent during my formative first two years, especially in Sensor Technology and Embedded Systems, which were technically complex topics, were extremely valuable for making an effective connection between theoretical knowledge and its practical application. With great effort, I managed to gain significant practical skills in working with important test equipment, such as digital oscilloscopes, multimeters, and different microcontroller boards. Identifying and fixing problems in broken electronic circuits enhanced my analytical skills significantly.

Hardware failures of individual components while performing several critical and timely experiments often caused me to conduct the tests again and under severe time pressure. At first, this very difficult initial learning phase was quite discouraging for me and impacted my confidence negatively for a little while. As a remedy for this issue, I began reading through the complicated laboratory manuals prior to starting the experiment and carefully checking each connection in the circuits prior to turning the system on for the first time. Another step that I have developed is to save the simulation results from Proteus before actually assembling any real hardware. In the future, I hope to set up a full-fledged personal electronics laboratory at home to practice my skills.

Reflection 3: Communication & Presentation Skills – SPI Module

As for the Social and Professional Issues module, it made me learn how to speak about technical subjects to non-technical people. In addition, I practiced impromptu speaking skills that forced me to quickly come up with something interesting to say. This module helped me learn how to clearly explain technical concepts using appropriate language.

My nervousness in spontaneous assignments used to influence my fluency and coherence. Speaking before an audience was especially difficult during the first few weeks. I tried to practice speaking in front of a mirror and my peers to gain more confidence. The useful suggestions from my friends enabled me to figure out which parts I needed to work on, for instance, pacing and making eye contact. It is my plan to be part of a public speaking club as well as take other courses in technical communication.
